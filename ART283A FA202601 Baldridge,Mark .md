## Introduction

**From the catalog:**

The history of animation is the history of image making and performance — of photography, cinema, sound, computers and AI

Within this context we'll explore:  

* **Technologies** 

From cave painting to photography to motion pictures to animation, to computer simulation, each is based on a certain level of technology and until the technology exists, certain kinds of artifacts are difficult to imagine and may be impossible to execute

> The history of animation is a history of certain technologies

* **Techniques** 

Drawing every frame can be grueling and expensive, "cell" style and "limited" animation cut out some of the expense and labor, what this means for the quality of animation in an open question

> Techniques vary a great deal over time and artists may invent their own. Technique is in the manner of using the technologies at hand

* **People**

Certain individuals and groups are inextricably linked with the history of animation, whether as artists, innovators producers of project or "talent", the leave an indelible mark on the animation of their time and whatever follows

> People bring personality to the history of animation, whether they are colorful individuals who stand out, or quiet innovators behind the scenes

## Goals and Objectives

### Instructional Goals: 

* Learn how animation developed, grew and consider where it's going 
* Explore the relation people and technologies, which brings about technique 
* Develop ideas, theses and learn to express them in a cogent manner
* Imagine the future of animation 

### Learning Objectives

* **Knowledge** Become conversant with the history of animation, know the names, the techniques and the tech that makes it possible
* **Conceptual** Become able to identify themes, similarities, difference between artists, styles, eras animation
* **Developmental** Learn to express your informed thoughts on the subject of animation and its past, develop critical apparatus for discussing the challenges and merits of animated works

### Projects

In this course we will produce three Projects

These may be creative, analytical, or expository/critical, or advanced that is:

* **Creative** might include making an animation of your own; writing a screenplay for one; composing and recording "cartoon music"

* **Analysis** could take the shape of a video, comparing the styles of different directors of animation or the work of one director for several studios; demonstrating how a particular technology works, writing an essay on the life and or works of a person important to the history of animation
 
* **Exposition** includes offering factual accounts or explanations, criticism compares and judges; one might expound on how sync sound changed animation or critique an animation or animation studio

* **Advanced** Consider taking your projects to a new level, something publishable, competitive or prize-worthy. Advanced projects must be submitted to some external contest, publisher or festival
