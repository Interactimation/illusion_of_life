# Two & Three Color and "Single Strip" processes

**Technicolor** is a brand name and as such applies to a number of processes for adding color to film

We've already seen hand-painted films like [Buterfly Dance from 1900](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=paUoddLZ7GQ) and _dyed_ films, such as [The Adventures of Prince Achmed](https://animationobsessive.substack.com/p/the-99-year-old-film-that-stops-people)

And dying (or "tinting") will be a major part of Technicolor for some time

## Two Color Process
Of the [primary colors of light](https://learn.leighcotnoir.com/artspeak/elements-color/primary-colors/) (which are different from those of pigment) only red and green at first are used, in what is known as the [Two Color Process](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8iy_MjegGWY)

Notice that the film itself is still black and white, only the separations of green and red are dyed and _glued together!_

## Three Color Process

The later included all the primaries (Red, Green, Blue) in a [Three Color Process](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N-T8MVrw1L0) —more complicated and more successful, but still including various black and white layers being dyed or "tinted"

## Eastman "Single Strip"

And last, in 1950, came [Eastman Color](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=frVr9icjm4c&t=28s) which kept the the color information on a single strip of film, finally supplanting Technicolor
