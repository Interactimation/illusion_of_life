# Motion Pictures

Animation, as we understand it, begins with the invention of cinema or "motion pictures."

There were various kinds of moving pictures, as we've seen, before the breakthrough, and this came about in pretty short order:

## Edison

The Edison Company publicly demonstrated the [Kinetoscope](https://www.pbs.org/video/american-experience-kinetoscope/) 1893 and it was an overnight success with public parlours established around the world!

## Lumière

The first to present projected moving pictures to a paying audience were the Lumière brothers in Paris, 1895

They their own invention, the  [Cinématographe](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dE4WMh-ma8E), was a camera, a projector and a film printer all in one!

So not only did they first project "moving pictures" on a screen, they also put human-powered, mobile camera/projection technology in the hands of amateurs around the world...

One of these was:

## Méliès

Georges Méliès was a stage magician who got interested in if filmmaking and invented some of the first "special effects" like the double exposure the matte shot and his first, the notably, the ["stop trick"](https://youtu.be/L8is28gAOTc?t=103), and all of these were in common use right up until the advent of CGI!

Méliès made over 500 films, some of them pretty famous, like _Le Voyage dans la Lune_ (A Trip to the Moon) (1902) which features [the man in the moon getting a rocket in the eye](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8fG9UF16i6M)

> **Consider:** Is it weird, after seeing that, to think someone might have easily seek it as a kid and lived to the real moon landing, in 1969?

I want you to see a complete short film by Méliès, [_Le Diable noir_ (The Black Imp)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OEwRIn4lSWw) made in 1905. I choose it for several reasons: 
It features the "stop trick" pretty heavily, it has some of the energy and pretend physics of later cartoons and _it's clearly a romp!_  

> **Consider:** The music you'll year in The Black Imp is not part of the original film. Sound in cinema was a long time coming, included many and various schemes and we'll introduce _sync-sound_ until the late 1920s! In the mean time, try watching it with any fast paced, bouncy music you like...

> **Note:** Fewer than 200 Méliès films remain, and many other movies of the era are "lost" films. This is partly due to the corrosive and [highly flammable](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/FxTKLhqAmbM) nature of early "nitrate" film stock

## Winsor McCay

McCay was already a famous cartoonist in 1914 when he changed careers for a bit and became one of the worlds first animators! 

He'd made his name as the creator of [_Little Nemo in Slumberland_](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/9e/Little_Nemo_Walking_Bed_detail.jpg?utm_source=commons.wikimedia.org&utm_campaign=index&utm_content=original) which began as a full-page color strip in the _New York Herald_

> This remarkable strip was essentially a one-gag project. Most episodes close as Nemo falls out of bed, ending the dream

He'd experimented with animation a bit before producing [_Gertie the Dinosaur_ (1914)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NDrAjHeQMf4)

A show he took on the road to vaudeville theatres and other theatrical venues where he would follow a script to _"interact"_ with the giant 
brontosaurus! 

Even, at the climax, _entering the frame of the film!_
